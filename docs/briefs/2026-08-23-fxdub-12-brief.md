## 2. The hard case, named

The scene is four lines in 10.06 s. **Three of the four belong to VOICE, who is off-frame.**

```
VOICE (off-frame):  Hey, how's it going?
MAC   (on-frame):   Not bad. Can't complain.          <- enters at 2.30 s
VOICE:              Good to hear, good to hear.
VOICE:              Hey, tell Charlie I got that thing for him, whenever he wants to drop by.
```

Two readings of why this is the hard case:

1. **Whose mouth.** Florence's caption of the picture is *"two men standing in a city at night,
   facing each other."* Feed the full VO stem to a lip-sync node and it will drive some face for
   **roughly 7 of 10 seconds of dialogue belonging to a character who is not in the shot.** Every
   audio check we own stays green while the picture is ruined — the exact defect class fx-dub was
   built for, transposed into the visual domain.
2. **Faces in profile at 480p.** Two men facing *each other* are in profile to camera, at
   832 x 480, in a generated night exterior. Kling's node text demands *"a distinct face"*;
   sync.so's claims it *"handles close-ups, profiles and obstructions automatically."* If that
   claim is why you flagged the difficulty, say so plainly and tell us how it degrades.

Our intended answer to (1) is that we **never feed the mix**. `fxdub-dialogue --only-speaker MAC`
already produces a per-character stem carrying MAC's line and *silence* everywhere else. That
stem is what goes to `SyncLipSyncNode.audio`, and `model.speaker_selection` gets pinned to
`coordinates` on MAC's face — never `default`, never `auto-detect`. **Tell us if that is wrong.**

## 3. The duration decision is already load-bearing

From the schema, `model.sync_mode` sets the output length:

| mode | output length | effect on our 161 frames |
|---|---|---|
| `bounce` / `loop` / `remap` | = audio length | picture altered — `remap` time-stretches it |
| `cut_off` | = shorter track | frames lost |
| **`silence`** | = longer track, shorter one padded | **161 frames survive** |

Our mix is 10.000 s of content against a 10.0625 s picture, so **only `silence` preserves the
deliverable**. Our existing container receipt already asserts *161 frames intact* and would fail
the other four modes — the first good news in this round. **Confirm by execution that `silence`
behaves as its tooltip says.**
