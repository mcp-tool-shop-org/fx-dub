## Research grounding (the dispatch's empirical floor)

Five lanes, dispatched 2026-08-21, all findings retrieval-backed (agents quoted fetched text; per-lane `tool_uses` receipts: 18/28/24/38/29). Citation identifiers verified per Step 4 — see `2026-08-21-fxdub-v1.dispatch.verify.md`.

### Lane 1 — temporal alignment (caption-mediated vs video-conditioned)

1. **Caption-mediated V2A measurably loses temporal alignment; an energy/loudness envelope beats a caption as the bridge.** Jeong, Kim, Chun & Lee 2024 (arXiv:2407.05551). ReWaS vs caption/latent-mediated Seeing&Hearing: AV-align 0.3008 vs 0.1858, FAD 2.16 vs 6.58.
2. **DeSync (Synchformer-predicted offset, seconds) + ImageBind score are the field-standard V2A metrics; even SOTA video-conditioned models sit at ~80–110 ms offset.** Cheng, Ishii, Hayakawa, Shibuya, Schwing & Mitsufuji 2024 (arXiv:2412.15322). VGGSound: MMAudio 0.081 s … FoleyCrafter 0.109 s.
3. **Text conditioning is a semantic channel, not a temporal one** — dropping text moves MMAudio's DeSync only 0.081→0.086. Same source (Table 4). Better captions will not buy timing.
4. **Naive chunk-and-concatenate fragments audio; transformer V2A degrades past its training window.** Simon et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.20981): "disjointed transitions, unaligned sound events"; 3–4 point FD/IB drop beyond the 8 s window.
5. **Per-scene generation then concatenation beat whole-clip for a short-window base model; segments <2 s are invalid.** Yi & Li 2024 (arXiv:2409.09823): FD 14.65→10.74 with CLIP change-point segmentation.
6. **Boundary artifacts are fixable with overlapping windows + latent blending + a global reference.** Jiang et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.08557): ~10 s overlapping segments, "seamless transitions," 26 s long-form FAD 1.85.
7. **Layered, one-sound-at-a-time generation (Foley-artist style) outperforms single-pass mixed output.** Hayakawa, Ishii, Shibuya & Mitsufuji 2026 (arXiv:2506.20995): sequential per-track synthesis with negative audio guidance.
8. **Onset/timestamp control belongs in a dedicated module and composes with text prompts.** Zhang et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.01494); shot detection is solved and cheap — TransNet V2, Souček & Lokoč 2020 (arXiv:2008.04838).

### Lane 2 — caption → audio-prompt transformation

9. **User prompts are systematically under-specified vs T2A training captions ("audionese"); LLM rewriting improves alignment.** Chang et al. 2023 (arXiv:2311.00897).
10. **Rewriting works best as retrieval of training-caption exemplars, not free-form paraphrase.** Chang et al. 2023 (arXiv:2311.00895).
11. **Visual detail with no audible counterpart DEGRADES T2A output — the rewrite stage's primary job is subtractive filtering.** Yuan et al. 2024–25 (arXiv:2407.04416): Sound-VECaps "removes visual-only information and contains only audible contents"; audio-only prompts scored best (FAD 0.96).
12. **A text intermediate improves V2A only if it is an AUDIO caption, not a visual one.** Liu, Su & Shlizerman 2024 (arXiv:2411.05679): KLD 2.25→1.41 with audio-caption conditioning; LLaVA visual captions underperform.
13. **Decomposing V2A into image→text and text→audio is a validated architecture** (with a sound-vocabulary prior needed in the bridge). Xie, Yu, He & Li 2024, CVPR (arXiv:2401.04394).
14. **Stable Audio's own prompting guide specifies prose with a three-slot schema: source + action + production/room character.** Stability AI prompting guide (github.com/Stability-AI/stable-audio-3 · docs/guides/prompting.md).
15. **Sentence-form beats bare tag lists for CLAP-conditioned systems, modestly** (46.8 vs 45.3 R@1). Wu et al. 2022 (arXiv:2211.06687).
16. **Near-synonym prompt edits shift acoustics even when embeddings agree — a nondeterministic rewriter destroys reproducibility.** Wu 2026 (arXiv:2603.13824).
17. **Vision-only captioners are weak proxies for audible content** (removing audio cost 83% CIDEr vs 30% for vision in audio-visual captioning). Ishikawa et al. 2025 (arXiv:2510.24024).
18. *(Advisory, provenance-caveat — ablation numbers from search index, not direct fetch; NOT load-bearing)* Motion-aware video captions beat still-frame+rewrite in Movie Gen's text-to-video ablations. Meta 2024 (arXiv:2410.13720); Qwen2.5-VL claims second-level event localization (Bai et al. 2025, arXiv:2502.13923). No retrievable head-to-head exists for downstream **audio** quality.

### Lane 3 — synthetic-voice governance

19. **EU AI Act Art. 50(2): the PROVIDER obligation is machine-readable marking of synthetic audio** ("marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated"). EC AI Act Service Desk, Art. 50 (ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50).
20. **Art. 50 applies from 2 August 2026** (already in force); grace only for systems placed on market before that date, to 2 Dec 2026. EC FAQ (digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act).
21. **Art. 50(4): the user PUBLISHING a deepfake-class dub carries the disclosure duty** — "clear and distinguishable… at the latest at the time of the first interaction"; lighter for evidently artistic/fictional works. Same sources.
22. **Tennessee's ELVIS Act reaches the TOOL: liability for distributing software whose "primary purpose or function" is producing an identifiable individual's voice.** Tenn. Code Ann. § 47-25-1101 et seq. (eff. 2024-07-01); Proskauer analysis 2024.
23. **AI voices in robocalls are illegal without prior consent (US).** FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (2024-02-08, docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf).
24. **NO FAKES Act (S.4591) is pending, not law** — federal digital-replica right with notice-and-takedown; reported to Senate 2026-06-24. govinfo BILLSUM-119s4591.
25. **YouTube requires a disclosure toggle for realistic synthetic content but EXEMPTS cloning your own voice for dubs.** support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491.
26. **TikTok auto-labels from C2PA Content Credentials (Steering Committee member July 2026); C2PA 2.2 supports MP3/WAV.** c2pa.org + C2PA 2.2 spec (2025-05-01).
27. **Both candidate watermarkers are MIT (AudioSeal incl. weights; Perth ships in every Chatterbox output), but robustness is defeatable — defense in depth, not watermark-alone.** Fernandez et al., ICML 2024 (arXiv:2401.17264); resemble-ai/chatterbox README; AudioMarkBench, Liu et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.06979); Özer et al. 2025 (arXiv:2505.19663) — neural compression is the hardest attack; Ding et al. 2026 (arXiv:2606.22310) — adaptive attack reaches 0% detection.

### Lane 4 — mix & loudness standards

28. **ITU-R BS.1770-5 (Nov 2023) is the in-force loudness/true-peak algorithm; name the revision in the manifest** (Netflix still gates dialogue on 1770-1). itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770-5-202311-I.
29. **EBU R128 v5.0: −23 LUFS ±0.5 LU is the BROADCAST anchor, not the online default** (s1 short-form, s2 streaming, s4 cinematic). tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128.
30. **ATSC A/85: −24 LKFS ±2 measured on the ANCHOR ELEMENT — dialogue when present.** atsc.org A/85:2013. → anchor fx-dub's mix to the dialogue bus.
31. **Netflix near-field: −27 LKFS ±2 DIALOG-GATED, true peak ≤ −2 dBTP; QC accepts −24 ±3 full-mix (1770-4).** partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com (Sound Mix Specs v1.6, 2024). → report BOTH gated and ungated numbers.
32. **Streaming normalization: Spotify −14 LUFS (TP −1/−2 dBTP), YouTube ≈ −14 attenuate-only; AES TD1008 recommends −18 LUFS for speech+music+effects ("Assorted") content, TP −1 dBTP.** Spotify loudness page; productionadvice.co.uk/stats-for-nerds; aes.org TD1008 (2021). → **−18 LUFS integrated / −1 dBTP is the best-evidenced default for mixed dub content.**
33. **Ducking depth: listeners prefer dialogue ≥10 LU over music, ≥15 LU over ambience; experts under-duck by ~4 LU vs non-experts; DPP floor is 4 LU speech-to-background, 0 LU = critical alarm.** Torcoli et al., JAES 2019 (DOI 10.17743/jaes.2019.0052); Torcoli et al., AES 156 (arXiv:2405.17364).
34. **Preferred dialogue-to-background difference varies ~5.7 LU between listeners — ship the offset as a knob, defaulted loud.** Resti et al., QoMEX 2023 (arXiv:2305.19100).
35. **No authoritative spec exists for ducking time constants — ship labelled folklore defaults (attack ≈20 ms, release ≈600 ms) and let the SBLD metric do the gating.** (Vendor/practitioner sources only; absence verified.)
36. **Precedent for an LRA target + mono offset in a shipped spec: Google Assistant −16 LUFS stereo / −19 mono, TP −1.5, LRA 11.** developers.google.com/assistant/tools/audio-loudness.

## Architecture lock (each choice traces to findings by number)

**A. The pipeline is caption-mediated by design — and honest about what that buys.** Image→text→audio decomposition is a validated architecture whose value is swappable, license-clean stages (13, and the KB license wall: every commercially-clean audio model is text-conditioned; every direct-V2A open model is non-commercial). The cost is temporal: captions carry semantics, not timing (1, 3). **v1 therefore targets ambience/dialogue-grade sync and says so**; impact-grade foley timing is a v1.5+ event-timeline feature (8), never a "better captions" feature (3).

**B. Three-layer output: bed + spot effects + dialogue, mixed from stems — never a single generated mix.** (7, 38, 44.) v1 ships bed + dialogue; the spot-effects layer lands with the event timeline (8). Every layer is saved as a stem alongside the mix.

**C. Bed generation: one window up to 60 s; segment longer videos at shot boundaries with overlap + crossfade; minimum segment 2 s.** ACE-Step's measured production ceiling is 60 s (readouts prompt-lab); beyond one window, naive concatenation fragments (4), segmented generation wins for short-window models (5), and overlap+blend repairs boundaries (6). Shot boundaries come from TransNet V2/PySceneDetect-class detection (8) — host-side in v1.x, not in-graph.

**D. An explicit caption→audio-prompt rewrite stage is mandatory, deterministic, and SUBTRACTIVE-first.** Direct wiring of a visual caption into T2A tags is the measured failure mode (11, 12, 17); rewriting toward training-caption register works (9, 10). The rewriter: strips non-audible visual detail, names sound sources/actions/room in prose (14, 15), runs at temperature 0 with pinned model+prompt (16), and caches its output in the manifest. v1: host-side via local Ollama, with a manual text-box fallback on cloud. The Florence stage itself pins `do_sample=false`, fixed seed (16; house determinism rules).

**E. Dialogue is authored, never auto-generated from the caption.** A scene description is not a script (KB standing rule; 21 makes the publisher accountable for what the voice says). The graph ships a must-edit placeholder; the script is a human checkpoint.

**F. Mix defaults (all exposed as knobs, all reported in the manifest):** integrated target **−18 LUFS** (AES TD1008 "Assorted", 32), true-peak ceiling **−1 dBTP** (32), dialogue anchor with bed offset **−15 LU under dialogue for ambience, −10 LU for music** (30, 33), biased toward the louder-dialogue non-expert preference (33) and adjustable because preference spread is ~5.7 LU (34). All branches resample to the **48 kHz studio standard** before the bus (measured: ACE 1.0 decodes 44.1 k stereo, ChatterBox cloud decodes 24 k MONO — a raw `AudioCombine` of those is a defect, see the as-built ledger). Manifest reports: BS.1770 revision (28), integrated + dialogue-gated LUFS (31), max true peak, LRA (36), per-window speech-to-background difference with count of windows < 4 LU (33). Ducking constants ship as labelled folklore defaults (35).

**G. Governance is a feature: consent gate, watermark stance, disclosure docs, provenance manifest.** Voice cloning is local-only (cloud has no audio upload — KB) and gated on documented consent; fx-dub ships NO person-specific voice packs (22). The README states: Perth watermarking stays on (27 — and stripping it undercuts the user's own Art. 50 position, 19); publishers carry the disclosure duty (21, 25 — including YouTube's own-voice exemption nuance); telephony/robocall use is prohibited (23); the manifest carries a C2PA-shaped provenance block (26) recording models, seeds, graph hash, and watermark presence — which is also the Art. 50(2) machine-readable mark (19, 20). Watermarks alone are defeatable (27), so the layered manifest+watermark+consent design is deliberate.

**H. Deliverable set (the "dub kit"):** `dubbed.mp4` (audio muxed back onto the source video — the deliverable nobody in the Comfy ecosystem ships, 43), `mix.flac` (48 kHz master), `stems/` (bed, dialogue, later spots — 38, 44), `caption.txt` + `audio_prompt.txt` (the semantic intermediates; house rule: STRING outputs reach the manifest via SaveText), `manifest.json` (loudness report F + provenance block G). SRT export follows with multi-line dialogue (38).

**I. Model lanes (from the KB, decisive axis = license):** publishable default = **ACE-Step 1.5** (MIT) bed + **Chatterbox** (MIT) dialogue + **Florence-2** (MIT) describe. Documented conditional lane = **Stable Audio 3** SFX (Community License < $1M + Gemma rider). Documented local/non-commercial lane = direct V2A (**MMAudio** CC-BY-NC weights, etc.) for users who want sync over shippability — with the honest note that it beats caption-mediation on sync by construction (1, 2). Cloud VO alternative: **Qwen3-TTS** (Apache-2.0) preset/designed voices.

**J. Verification is external at every layer** (the studio's standing pattern): graphs must be static-validation green before delivery; audio is verified by BS.1770 meters and the SBLD metric (33), not by the generating model; citations in this dispatch were gated through a different-family verifier before the lock above was written.
