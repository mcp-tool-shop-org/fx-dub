# prompt-craft `domains/audio` — study-swarm dispatch (architecture proposal)

**Date:** 2026-08-21 · **Status:** proposal, Director-approved to design; **not yet built, not yet canon in the fx-dub dispatch.**
**Protocol:** research-grounded-advisor ("study-swarm"), 4 parallel retrieval-backed lanes (per-lane `tool_uses` 34 / 35 / 26 / 23; every finding carries a quoted phrase from a fetched source).
**Target:** a new domain plugin in `prompt-craft` (MIT, v1.0.0, `E:/AI/prompt-craft`), registered exactly the way `core/plugin.py` documents: *"Adding `video` or `workflow` is one `register` call; `core/` never changes."* A plugin supplies a **Generator**, **tiered Verifiers keyed 0/1/2**, and **encoder-craft rules**.
**Consumer:** fx-dub's caption→audio-prompt stage (dispatch decision D), today specified only as "a deterministic subtractive rewrite."

> ⚠ **Step-4 external verification has NOT been run on these citations yet.** The fx-dub v1 dispatch was gated through `roleos verify-citations` → prism before its architecture lock; this one must be too before any finding here becomes canon. Treat every number below as retrieval-backed-but-ungated. **That gate is the first task of the session that builds this.**

## Research grounding

### Lane 1 — the atom vocabulary

1. **AudioSet's ontology is structurally ideal and legally unusable for us.** Gemmeke et al. 2017, github.com/audioset/ontology — 632 classes, depth 6, typed JSON per entry. But: *"The ontology is made available by Google Inc. under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0) license."* Vendoring the JSON imports ShareAlike into an MIT tool. **Deriving a term list and citing it does not.**
2. **UCS is public domain and professionally adopted, but it is a filing system, not a claim schema.** v8.2, *"82 main categories and 753 subcategories"*, *"As a public domain resource, it's available for anyone to use"* — its mandatory fields are `CatID_FXName_CreatorID_SourceID`, i.e. provenance, with no environment/perspective/envelope slots.
3. **AudioSet leaf classes are measurably ambiguous.** Fonseca et al., FSD50K, arXiv:2010.00475 — *"some AudioSet class descriptions were found to be ambiguous, allowing multiple interpretations"*; FSD50K kept 200 of 632 and merged sparse leaves into parents. Evidence for a **small** closed vocabulary over wholesale adoption.
4. **Visual-only claims actively degrade generated audio — the exact fx-dub risk.** Yuan et al., Sound-VECaps, arXiv:2407.04416 — at inference *"the system that uses prompts without visual-only features (AudioCaps-Enhanced_A) generates the best result (0.96 on FAD)"*, beating the visually-enriched caption. **The atom layer must drop non-audible claims, not carry them.**
5. **A validated four-slot audible schema already exists.** Sun et al., Auto-ACD, arXiv:2309.11500 — captions target *"the 'what' … the 'who' … the 'how' … and the 'where' - the location the sound occurs"*, and it is *"the only audio-language dataset that encompasses environmental information."*
6. **Audio-caption corpora deliberately withhold vision because it corrupts audible description.** Drossos et al., Clotho, arXiv:1910.09387 — *"During annotation no other information but the audio signal was available to the annotators, e.g. video or word tags."* fx-dub inverts this by construction, so **verification must be audio-grounded, never caption-grounded.**
7. **The failure mode is named in the literature.** Audio-Language Datasets survey, arXiv:2407.06947v2 — *"sounds in videos may originate from off-screen events"*, and captions *"describe visually observable details irrelevant to the audio, such as 'the man is wearing a blue sweater.'"* → atoms need an explicit `audible` gate and an on/off-screen flag.
8. **Stable Audio's own guidance is three structured slots, not prose.** Stability AI `stable-audio-3/docs/guides/prompting.md` — source (*"exactly what object, instrument, or synthesizer is making the sound?"*), action (*"how is the sound being triggered, and how long does it last?"*), production (*"where is the mic placed, and what's the room character?"*), plus the literal `TrackType: SFX` tag.
9. **Temporal slots are the known weak axis; caption precision beats caption volume.** Xie et al., AudioTime, arXiv:2407.02857 — *"temporal relationships … are currently underrepresented in mainstream models."* Haji-Ali et al., arXiv:2406.19388 — *"increasing the dataset size at a lower caption quality does not yield similar gains even at a bigger model."*

### Lane 2 — the verifier

10. **Both candidate audio-text encoders are license-clean for an MIT plugin.** LAION-CLAP, Wu et al., arXiv:2211.06687 — *"the model achieves state-of-the-art performance in the zero-shot setting"*; code CC0-1.0, HF weights Apache-2.0, ~158M params (consumer-GPU trivial). Microsoft MS-CLAP — *"The project is available under an MIT license."*
11. **The industry-standard CLAP-score for generated audio pins our config.** Stability AI `stable-audio-metrics/src/clap_score.py` — default checkpoint `630k-audioset-fusion-best.pt`, and *"sample rate should be 48000"* — matching our 48 kHz runtime standard exactly. Note it reports a mean cosine: **a ranking metric, not a detector.**
12. **⚠ Absolute CLAP cosine is NOT a thresholdable probability.** Acevedo, Rocamora & Fuentes 2025, arXiv:2506.04376 — *"text anchors prototypes (red) have the highest cosine distances, indicating poor alignment with audio samples"*; text anchors sit farther from their own class's audio than other classes' centroids do. **Calibrate per-claim against a held-out audio bank; never a global constant.**
13. **Cross-query comparability is not fixable by mean-subtraction.** Zhu et al., COMET, arXiv:2605.29628 — *"only a small, interpretable subset of axes … contributes substantially to similarity computation"*, and *"the mean component represents only partially the modality gap."*
14. **Prompt phrasing swings accuracy; entropy is the abstain signal.** El Khoury et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.05011 — *"small variations leading to large fluctuations in accuracy"*, *"substantial variability to prompt selection for all datasets."* → per-claim **paraphrase ensembles**, with high ensemble entropy meaning *escalate*, not *verdict*.
15. **⛔ Negation collapses. Forbid negative claims at tier 1.** Kuan & Lee 2026, arXiv:2607.12290 — *"Negation accuracy collapses to 0.2–1.1%"* on AudioCaps against 25% chance, 1.9–7.1% on Clotho, across five LAION-CLAP checkpoints, M2D-CLAP and WAVE-7B. (This rhymes exactly with our own vision-side A2 finding that negation foils don't bind in CLIP-family text space.)
16. **⛔ Temporal claims must abstain, even at tier 2.** arXiv:2606.17417 — audio-LLMs *"often under-utilize audio when textual cues are available"*; the best intervention moved accuracy only *"from 55.9% to 59.1%."*
17. **CLAP genuinely encodes reverb and loudness, but not brightness.** Martel et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.03806 — *"RT60 is strongly and nearly linearly encoded"* (R²≥0.67), *"LUFS is also strongly and approximately linearly encoded"* (R²≥0.76), while spectral centroid *"performs at or below chance on four of five datasets."* → **tier 0 owns level, silence and brightness; CLAP may carry room and loudness claims.**
18. **A cheap MIT closed-set tagger beats CLAP at "is class X present."** Schmid et al., EfficientAT, arXiv:2211.04772 — MIT, `mn10_as` 4.88M params / 47.1 mAP. Compare PANNs (Apache-2.0) and BEATs (MIT). **Audio Flamingo 3 is disqualified** — *"The model is released under the NVIDIA OneWay Noncommercial License."* Commercially-safe tier-2 audio-LLM: `Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct`, Apache-2.0.

### Lane 3 — caption → typed atoms

19. **The "structured output hurts quality" claim was rebutted on methodology.** Tam et al. 2024, arXiv:2408.02442 reported *"a significant decline in LLMs reasoning abilities under format restrictions"*; the dottxt response shows the paper *"confuses structured generation with JSON-mode"* (a fine-tuning behaviour with *"no guarantees about the returned value"*), and matched-prompt re-runs give NL 0.68 / JSON-unstructured 0.73 / **JSON-structured 0.77**.
20. **Independently, constrained decoding helps.** Geng et al., JSONSchemaBench, arXiv:2501.10868 — *"Constrained decoding consistently improves the performance of downstream tasks up to 4%, even for tasks with minimal structure like GSM8k."*
21. **When constraint does hurt, the cause is tokenizer misalignment, not constraint.** Beurer-Kellner et al., DOMINO, ICML 2024, arXiv:2403.06988 — engines *"significantly impair task accuracy, if they do not correctly align the underlying LLM sub-word vocabularies with external constraints"*; the aligned implementation reaches *"almost 2× speedup over unconstrained decoding."*
22. **The closed-roster pattern is published and input-dependent grammars are supported.** Geng et al., GCD, EMNLP 2023 — guarantees *"that the output follows a given structure"* with *"the grammar to depend on the input"*. De Cao et al., GENRE, arXiv:2010.00904 — prefix-trie decoding makes out-of-roster entities un-emittable.
23. **⚠ Temperature 0 is NOT a determinism guarantee — do not claim one.** He, Thinking Machines 2025 — *"the primary reason nearly all LLM inference endpoints are nondeterministic is that the load (and thus batch-size) nondeterministically varies"*; 80 unique completions from 1000 identical temp-0 requests, versus *"all of our 1000 completions are identical"* only with batch-invariant kernels. Atil et al., arXiv:2408.04667 quantify it — *"accuracy variations up to 15%"* — and give the metric to report instead: **TARr@N / TARa@N agreement rates.**
24. **Extraction stages hallucinate entities even at temperature 0; abstention is the mitigation.** Vargas et al. 2025, arXiv:2506.08827 — *"LLaMA-2 7b, even with zero temperature, shows a significant number of hallucinations in extractions."*
25. **Language models lack auditory commonsense — the visual→audible mapping must be authored or retrieved.** Ok et al., AudioBERT/AuditoryBench, arXiv:2409.08199 — *"we discover that language models severely lack auditory commonsense knowledge"*; retrieval augmentation improved results by over 40%. **VGGSound** (Chen et al., ICASSP 2020) supplies a ready closed label set where *"the sound source is visually evident"* — the natural join target.

### Lane 4 — re-roll economics

26. **Repeated sampling scales only where a real verifier exists.** Brown et al., arXiv:2407.21787 — *"In domains without automatic verifiers … majority voting and reward models plateau beyond several hundred samples and fail to fully scale with the sample budget."*
27. **⚠ An imperfect verifier caps accuracy regardless of budget, and optimal N is small.** Stroebl et al., arXiv:2411.17501 — *"resampling cannot decrease this probability, so it imposes an upper bound to the accuracy … regardless of compute budget"*; *"Optimal sampling attempts are often fewer than 10."*
28. **Best-of-N against a proxy score is a Goodhart machine.** Gao, Schulman & Hilton, arXiv:2210.10760 — *"optimizing its value too much can hinder ground truth performance, in accordance with Goodhart's law."*
29. **⛔ THE DECISIVE AUDIO FINDING: CLAP best-of-N demonstrably hacks the verifier.** Jung et al., SCORE, arXiv:2509.19831 — *"using the CLAP model as a reward improves the CLAP score by 10.8% but increases the PQ score by only 1.4%"*, and *"BON with PQ reward attains a lower AQAScore than naive sampling despite a higher CLAP score, an evidence of verifier hacking."* Fix is a **composite of independent rewards**.
30. **CLAP ranking still helps — as preference, never as a pass/fail gate.** Hung et al., TangoFlux, arXiv:2412.21037 — *"TTA lacks structured mechanisms like verifiable rewards or gold-standard answers available for Large Language Models"*, yet CLAP-ranked preference optimisation improved both objective and subjective results.
31. **Self-correction without external feedback fails, and re-rolling beats it.** Huang et al., arXiv:2310.01798 — *"LLMs struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at times, their performance even degrades."* Kamoi et al., TACL 2024 — *"no prior work demonstrates successful self-correction with feedback from prompted LLMs"*; *"self-correction works well in tasks that can use reliable external feedback."*
32. **⚠ Standard audio metrics correlate weakly with human judgement.** Huang et al., arXiv:2503.16669 — *"nearly all existing metrics fail to effectively capture desiderata, and are only weakly correlated with human perception"* (MAD 0.62 vs FAD 0.14). Tailleur et al., arXiv:2403.17508 — VGGish embeddings *"yielded a correlation below 0.1."*
33. **Cascades pay, but only on calibrated confidence.** Chen, Zaharia & Zou, FrugalGPT, arXiv:2305.05176 — cascades *"match the performance of the best individual LLM… with up to 98% cost reduction."* Kotte 2026, arXiv:2605.18796 — *"most deployed routers use uncalibrated confidence scores and require per-workload threshold tuning"*; calibration (ECE 0.12→0.03) gave a 31% cost cut and *"threshold policies on the calibrated score are cost-optimal."*

## Proposed architecture (each choice traces to findings by number)

**A1 — Author our own closed vocabulary (~60–120 terms), don't vendor an ontology.** AudioSet's JSON is CC BY-SA and would infect an MIT plugin (1); UCS is public-domain but is a filing taxonomy without audible slots (2); AudioSet leaves are ambiguous and FSD50K already demonstrated that curating down beats adopting whole (3). Cite both as term *sources*. Join target for visually-evident sources: VGGSound's label set (25).

**A2 — The atom type is slot-structured, and every atom carries an `audible` gate.** Slots: `source · action · material · environment · perspective · envelope`, converging Auto-ACD's what/who/how/where (5) with Stable Audio's own source/action/production guidance (8). The `audible` flag plus an on/off-screen marker exists because visual-only detail measurably degrades output (4, 7) — **the atom layer's primary job is subtractive**, which is exactly what fx-dub decision D asserted without a mechanism.

**A3 — Extraction is constrained decoding whose schema enums ARE the closed vocabulary.** The "constraints hurt" objection is rebutted (19) and constrained decoding independently helps (20); real degradation comes from tokenizer misalignment, so the engine must be subword-aligned (21). Input-dependent grammars and prefix-trie rosters make an out-of-vocabulary atom un-emittable (22). LMs lack auditory commonsense, so the visual→audible mapping is an **authored table**, not model recall (25).

**A4 — We report a reproducibility MEASUREMENT, never a determinism claim.** Temperature 0 does not guarantee identical output (23), and extraction hallucinates even greedily (24). The plugin emits **TARa@N agreement across N runs** into the receipt, and caches the extraction so a pipeline replays from cache. *(This corrects a claim fx-dub's decision D currently makes too strongly.)*

**A5 — Tiered verifiers, with hard prohibitions:**
- **Tier 0 (deterministic, free):** integrated LUFS, true peak, silence, duration, spectral centroid/brightness. Owns brightness specifically because CLAP is at-or-below chance on it (17). This is the tier fx-dub already has in-graph.
- **Tier 1 (LAION-CLAP and/or EfficientAT, license-clean — 10, 18):** **positive presence claims only**, each evaluated as a paraphrase ensemble with entropy-based abstention (14), scored against a **per-claim calibrated** threshold from a held-out bank, never a global constant (12, 13).
- **Tier 2 (Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, Apache-2.0 — 18):** escalation for what tier 1 abstains on.
- **⛔ Forbidden at every automated tier:** negated claims (15) and temporal-ordering claims (16). These route to tier-0 deterministic checks where possible, otherwise straight to the human checkpoint.

**A6 — The gate blocks; it does not auto-select, and it re-rolls at most a few times.** This is the sharpest finding of the swarm: CLAP-driven best-of-N **provably hacks the verifier** in audio (29), proxy optimisation is Goodhart-prone (28), an imperfect verifier caps achievable accuracy no matter the budget with optimal N *"often fewer than 10"* (27), and audio metrics track human preference weakly (32). So: **N ≤ 4**, selection by a **composite** of independent signals (29, 30) never a single CLAP score, and a persistent failure surfaces to the human rather than burning credits — which is precisely prompt-craft's existing repair-ladder-plus-checkpoint shape. Self-refinement is excluded outright (31).

**A7 — Cost routing is gated on calibration.** Escalating tier 1 → tier 2 (or spending another generation credit) requires a calibrated confidence; uncalibrated thresholds need per-workload tuning and are not cost-optimal (33).
